Summary

What I found

  • Every process document says it is the "baseline for the LG-ERP conversion." Your Management Systems team is preparing these processes for Luis's system, not for this one. Two ERP projects are aiming at the same goal. That is the first thing management has to settle.
  • Most of what your people do inside Business Central today is already in our ERP, because we copied Business Central. Of the 35 process steps I mapped, 8 match, 7 are already supported but need your people to change how they work, and 6 are partly there.
  • The real gaps are the steps your people do outside Business Central: material issuance in Excel, fuel monitoring, pro forma deposit invoices, the NOSPA, scrap sales, the Cash Position Report, vendor accreditation and canvassing. 12 gaps in total.
  • LG-ERP is stronger at the front of Procure-to-Pay: vendor accreditation, multi-vendor RFQ with technical review, the Approved Service Report and 3-way match. These are good designs and we should adopt them.
  • LG-ERP is weak on accounting and controls. Its own code review says only payment runs post to the general ledger, credit notes change no amounts, permissions are mostly not checked, and at least 12 routes need no sign-in. Our ERP has the full Business Central posting engine, inventory costing, treasury, intercompany and foreign currency.
  • Our ERP is still a prototype. It runs on one PC, has no logins and no real database. Before go-live it needs both, whichever platform you choose.

Your Enterprise Requisition System (ERS)

ERS fills the biggest inventory gap

ERS lets an employee request items from the catalog. The department approver approves, Supply Chain reviews, then the items are released and stock goes down. That is the MRF/MIF issuance in CPD-SCW-002-01, which today lives in Excel and never reaches the books (gap #16). ERS also checks each request against the department budget (part of #3).

What to adopt as it is

  • The requestor screens. New Request with catalog search, My Requests, drafts, Request Changes, cancel and duplicate. Neither our ERP nor LG-ERP has screens this simple for ordinary employees.
  • Budget commitment. A request reserves budget while it is open and consumes it when released, with warning (80%) and critical (95%) thresholds and alerts. Our ERP only compares budget with actuals after posting.
  • Notifications and low-stock alerts to approvers and Supply Chain, and the 7 requisition reports.
  • Departments and cost centers, which map directly to the ERP's Department and Cost Center dimensions.

What has to change before it can connect to the ERP

  • Use the ERP item list. ERS has its own codes (ITEM-OS-0001) and one stock figure per item. It must use the Business Central item numbers and show stock per location and bin.
  • Know the company. ERS has no company field. It needs ADMIN, LAND, LLRI, LAMI and TANK, because each one has its own stock and books.
  • Post the release. Release should post an issue in the ERP, at average cost, to the expense account, department and equipment. Today it only lowers a number in ERS.
  • Turn shortages into purchases. If stock is short, Supply Chain should create a Purchase Requisition from the request in one click.
  • Route by workflow. The Asset and High Value (above ₱50,000) workflows are saved but not used yet. Every request takes the Standard route.
  • Budget by account, not only by category. ERS budgets are department × category at catalog price. The ERP budgets by G/L account and dimension. Use one budget source, the ERP's, and let ERS read it.
  • Add attachments, real logins and a shared database. Today ERS saves to one browser and anyone can switch users. Our ERP has the same limit.

How it fits

Make ERS the employee-facing requisition module of the one company ERP, not a separate system. Employees request in ERS. Released requests post to inventory in the ERP. Shortages become Purchase Requisitions that continue into Procure-to-Pay (canvass, PO, receipt, invoice, payment). Items, departments, cost centers, users and budgets come from the ERP master data, so nothing is typed twice.

ERS and our ERP are both plain browser apps built the same way, so joining them is straightforward. LG-ERP has its own Purchase Request module (M2), which overlaps with ERS. Include this in Decision 1.

Decision 1 of 15

Which system becomes the company ERP?

Running two ERP projects in parallel will cost twice and confuse users. Management should pick one of these, with Luis and Richard Coles in the room.

Option A

Our ERP is the platform

  • Keep the Business Central accounting we already built and tested.
  • Copy LG-ERP's good designs into it: accreditation, RFQ with technical review, ASR, 3-way match.
  • Still needs logins and a real database before go-live.
Option B

LG-ERP is the platform

  • Already has logins and a PostgreSQL database.
  • Must add everything outside Procure-to-Pay: posting engine, inventory costing, sales, treasury, intercompany, foreign currency, fixed assets.
  • Must fix the security findings in its own code review first.
My recommendation

One system, built from both

  • Use our ERP as the working specification for accounting, inventory, treasury and intercompany. It already behaves like Business Central and is tested.
  • Use LG-ERP's Procure-to-Pay screens and its production setup (logins, database).
  • One team, one codebase, one go-live plan.
